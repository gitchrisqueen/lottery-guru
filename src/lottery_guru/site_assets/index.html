An automated, honest lottery-prediction experiment

Null hypothesis: no strategy beats chance. Every arm is expected to score at chance, and the point of this experiment is to measure that convergence honestly. z-scores hovering near 0 are the correct result — nothing here is advice, and nothing here predicts winning numbers.

Reading this: z measures how far a strategy sits from pure chance, in standard deviations. Values bouncing around 0 mean it performs exactly as randomness predicts — the expected result. Cell shading grows with |z| in either direction; darker just means “further from chance so far,” not “winning.” It would take a sustained |z| > 3 over many draws to suggest anything real, and no strategy is expected to get there.

No tracked game has a capped-jackpot roll-down (the WinFall defect); unclaimed jackpots roll over, keeping per-ticket EV below cost. Digit-game payouts are fixed-odds — a structural house edge no monitor can wait out. “No opportunity” is the expected, and real, finding.
